Methodology

The score

{{ meta.score_formula }}

Two anchors, both measured on this part:

  • TSOL — the roofline: the time this workload would take if it were limited only by the arithmetic it must do and the bytes it must move. Nothing can beat it, so S = 1 is unreachable in practice.
  • Tb — the anchor: the fastest of four PyTorch formulations of the reference, measured on an idle GPU 0 under a locked clock. S = 0.5.

Where the bounds came from

What this board does not cover

One part, one clock. Every score here was measured on {{ meta.part or meta.device }} at FLOCK {{ meta.f_lock_mhz }} MHz. TSOL in milliseconds, Tb and FLOCK all differ by part, so a score measured on another part cannot be ranked against these — scripts/score_solutions.py refuses that rather than rescaling.

A second port to MI355X (FLOCK 1640, 8×1400 W) is in progress on feat/agent-scoreboard and is deliberately absent: its Tb is not yet anchor-verified, so its agent runs are scored on a weaker basis (headroom and speedup, not S) and it has no S to publish. When that lands it needs its own board, not extra rows on this one.

Problems whose bound is known to be wrong

A submitted kernel was measured faster than TSOL on these problems. Nothing beats the roofline, so this is a defect in the bound rather than a result. Those workloads are stored with no score so they cannot lift any ranking, and are still shown on the problem page.

More than one mechanism is involved, so no single cause is claimed here. Two are identified so far — the paged‑cache traffic defect (D18), where the declared‑traffic tier prices a KV cache at its full allocation while the kernel gathers only the pages kv_indices names; and D21, where a compute roofline came out ~15% slow on one problem and another was beaten by 0.3–1.3% on a bound with almost no headroom to begin with. Which applies to which is recorded in STATE.md, not inferred here.

Reading the leaderboard honestly

  • Benchmark score sums per-workload scores over every scoreable workload and counts anything not passed as zero. It is the headline because it cannot be improved by attempting fewer problems.
  • An asterisk on the benchmark score means the submission was not run on all {{ meta.scoreable_workloads }} scoreable workloads. The untested ones are still in the denominator, counting as zero exactly like failures, so a starred score is a floor rather than a measurement. Every row on the board is starred today.
  • Mean (attempted) divides by the workloads a submission attempted, and an attempt that failed counts as zero — it does not leave the average. Dividing by passes instead would put torch.compile (0.4907) above eager PyTorch (0.4548) purely because the 585 workloads it raises on would vanish from the denominator. It is never shown without coverage: on its own, any mean rewards attempting less.
  • Reference variants sit at 0.5 by construction. They define Tb; they are on the board to calibrate the scale, not to compete.
  • Flagged counts workloads the harness's anti-reward-hack check rejected — status == REWARD_HACK, an output accepted without the work being done. A flagged workload scores zero and is still displayed. The count is 0 across all 14,077 recorded results: the dash on every row means the check ran and caught nothing, not that it was never run. It is kept as a column precisely so that a first non-zero is visible the moment it happens.

The database is a view over the artifacts, rebuilt by leaderboard/ingest.py. Scores are computed with the repository's own sol_score, so the board cannot drift from the harness. Manifest {{ meta.git_sha }}.
